## Visual Fingerprint

- **Shape:** intentionally naive, slightly awkward silhouettes; gentle asymmetry, uneven geometric masses and simple but individual facial construction. Heads may be oval, long, broad or angular according to the person. Preserve the source's distinctive face outline and hair silhouette rather than imposing a universal round chibi head or fixed head/body ratio.
- **Face:** sparse, confident marks for eyes and mouth; a small simple triangular or block-like nose where supported by the reference; restrained warm cheek marks. Avoid realistic nose bridges, nostrils, airbrushed skin modeling and standardized giant glossy eyes. Simplification must still distinguish the people.
- **Line:** pressure-sensitive, intermittent colored-pencil or crayon contours; slight hand wobble and broken edges. Lines can emerge from the pigment rather than enclosing everything in a uniform heavy outline. Do not turn irregularity into random digital scratches.
- **Crayon / oil pastel:** broad waxy strokes with directional drag, uneven coverage, overlaps, opaque pigment buildup and small areas of paper showing through. Marks follow the shape of hair, fabric, foliage or architecture; they are not a noise layer pasted over clean digital forms.
- **Colored pencil:** selective fine hatch, dry edge and uneven pressure for details or contours. Let different media leave different mark sizes and opacity rather than one uniform grain scale everywhere.
- **Gouache:** matte, flatter opaque color masses, occasional dry-brush edges and visible layered overlaps. Keep the handmade relationship between broad painted shapes and smaller drawn marks; avoid smooth vector fills, plastic highlights or photoreal shading.
- **Paper:** warm off-white drawing-paper ground with plausible fibers/tooth visible through thin pigment and at dry stroke edges. Texture should interact with coverage and pressure. Avoid full-frame equal-density speckles, film grain, digital noise, distressed overlays, dirt or a “vintage filter” standing in for real material behavior.
- **Palette:** high-saturation color accents with a soft matte, retro harmony: warm red/orange, butter yellow, leaf green, cobalt/sky blue, pink and violet can coexist with cream and dark brown/charcoal. “Soft” means pigment edges, controlled contrast and color relationships, not desaturating everything to beige pastels or adding neon glow. These are examples, not mandatory scene objects or colors. Retain key source garment/motif colors so identity remains readable.
- **Space / light:** flattened, gently imperfect perspective, minimal volume, simple drawn shadows where needed. Preserve the photo's people, relative scale, gestures and recognisable setting; do not import the reference illustration's composition or decorative objects.
- **Background / sticker edge:** source-led simplified background for photo scenes; paper or a quiet field for requested avatars. No automatic white sticker border, decorative frame, cat, star, toy, lettering or storybook title.

## Check Before Acceptance

Inspect at overall size and close enough to see strokes:

1. Can each person be distinguished by face outline, hair, proportion and specific clothes?
2. Do shapes feel deliberately naive and varied, rather than six copies of one mascot face?
3. Do wax/pencil/paint marks have direction, pressure, coverage and layered edges that belong to the forms?
4. Does paper show through pigment naturally, instead of appearing as uniform noise across faces, clothes and background?
5. Are colors lively and retro with matte softness, while keeping source garment and accessory identities?
6. Does the image share the accepted keyframe's visual grammar while retaining only its own source scene?

A result with correct people but smooth digital shading plus a grain filter fails the material check. Repair the failed style dimension using the same active sources; do not invent props or redesign costumes to make the image feel more “storybook”.
